The competitive read

IndieAppStack does not compete with the tools it covers — it competes with everyone publishing about them. That set is dominated by tool vendors running content marketing (AppDrift, Adapty, RevenueCat, and a long tail of boilerplate and ASO vendors), plus demand aggregators (AlternativeTo, Product Hunt) with domain authority no new site can outrank soon.

The uncomfortable finding: the quadrant IndieAppStack assumes is empty is not empty. Its stated wedge is "honest 'when not to use this' calls a vendor cannot credibly publish" — but BuildMVPFast already publishes exactly that, and does it well, naming reliability problems, deprecations and pricing traps by vendor, then telling readers they may need none of the three. Neutrality of verdict is therefore not a defensible differentiator on its own.

What no competitor in this set does — not one — is make its claims checkable. Nobody sources a price. Nobody dates a figure. AppDrift prices the same competitor at $300, $500 and $5,000 per month on three of its own pages. AlternativeTo shows a six-week-old "last updated" stamp over a listing untouched for 22 months, and tells iOS developers the best RevenueCat alternative is Stripe — which Apple's IAP rules forbid. IndieAppStack already stamps every price with a check date. It has the differentiator built and is not claiming it.

If you own one thing Own verifiability, not neutrality. Be the only guide where every price is dated, sourced and auditable — written by a named human who will tell you not to buy, including when the affiliate link pays.

The two axes that actually decide who a solo developer trusts for a tool call: independence from the tools covered (horizontal) and willingness to reach a verdict rather than hand over a list (vertical).

The bottom-left is crowded and structurally stuck — vendors can publish deep content but never a verdict against themselves. The top-left is empty and uninhabitable. The top-right is the valuable ground, and IndieAppStack is not alone in it: BuildMVPFast sits right next to it, arriving from the services side.

The separation that remains is quality of evidence, not presence of opinion — which is why the recommended wedge moves from "we're neutral" to "we're checkable."

The real SOV finding is not the score — it is where the one hit sits

IndieAppStack's single citation is "best paywall tools for iOS apps," where it ranks #1. That is a genuine, defensible win and proof the model works.

Everything else is the problem. IndieAppStack has published competing content for ASO tools, Supabase vs Firebase, Superwall vs RevenueCat and privacy-friendly analytics — and was cited in none of them. The content exists; the visibility does not. This is a distribution and authority gap, not a content gap, and it changes what the next quarter should be spent on.

Worse, a branded search for "IndieAppStack" returns almost nothing it owns — the results fill with unrelated brands (IndieFeed, Indieflix, IndieGala, Indie App Santa, Indie App Catalog) and surface a single deep tool page. There are no third-party mentions at all. Since most AI citations come from earned sources rather than owned pages, this is the binding constraint on AI share of voice.

Whitespace & how to differentiate

01 — THE SHARPEST

Verifiability: the dimension nobody contests

Every player fails the same test. AppDrift contradicts its own competitor pricing by 16×, undated and unsourced. BuildMVPFast attaches no source or date to any figure. Adapty presents self-run, unaudited benchmarks and shows modified dates as publish dates. AlternativeTo publishes no prices at all, under a freshness stamp that misrepresents 22-month-old data. RevenueCat is methodologically transparent but refuses vendor comparison entirely.

IndieAppStack already ships the answer — every tool card carries "Pricing checked Jul 19, 2026" — and does not market it. Make it the masthead claim, publish a methodology page that commits to it, and link every price to its source.

02

Named human accountability

Of six players, exactly one does this well: RevenueCat, with 116 named authors. AppDrift bylines everything "AppDrift Team" with no founder named anywhere. AlternativeTo has no authors. BuildMVPFast presents one person as "multiple experts." Adapty names authors but hides the employer at the top byline.

IndieAppStack currently bylines "IndieAppStack" — sharing the weakest players' gap. This is the cheapest credibility upgrade available: a real name, a real reviewer, and Person schema instead of Organization.

03

The verdict nobody can write: "don't buy this"

Vendors structurally cannot say don't use us. BuildMVPFast cannot say don't hire us. Aggregators say nothing at all. But IndieAppStack can do something none of them can — advise against a tool it earns commission on, and say so explicitly on the page. That is the one editorial move in this market that cannot be copied, and it converts the affiliate model from a liability into proof.

04

Correct the errors the incumbents have left standing

AlternativeTo tells iOS developers the best RevenueCat alternative is Stripe, which App Store rules forbid for digital subscriptions. It has no Superwall entry at all, so nobody owns "Superwall alternatives." It files TelemetryDeck under web analytics. AppDrift's fabricated-looking $5,000 Sensor Tower figure is already circulating in AI answers.

Each is a high-intent, uncontested page with a built-in reason to be cited — and correcting a widely-repeated error is exactly the kind of content that earns links.

05

Earned mentions — the binding constraint on AI visibility

Most AI citations come from third-party sources, not owned pages. IndieAppStack has zero: a branded search returns unrelated companies and no press, no forum threads, no directory entries. Meanwhile BuildMVPFast and Adapty have engineered llms.txt and crawler policy, and IndieAppStack has neither.

One quiet advantage worth keeping: IndieAppStack's permissive robots.txt allows training crawlers that BuildMVPFast blocks. If assistants lean on parametric knowledge for tool recommendations, that is a long-run edge — do not copy BuildMVPFast's block.

06

Published-but-invisible: fix distribution before writing more

IndieAppStack has guides for ASO tools, Supabase vs Firebase, Superwall vs RevenueCat and privacy-friendly analytics, and was cited for none of them. Its one citation, "best paywall tools for iOS apps," is a #1 ranking — so the format works when it lands. The gap is authority and distribution, not output. Publishing more before fixing this compounds the problem.
