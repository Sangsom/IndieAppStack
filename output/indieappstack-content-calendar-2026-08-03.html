🗓 If you publish only one thing this quarter

“What the September 2026 App Store business terms actually cost a solo developer” — priced, dated, sourced, and shipped before the change lands.

From September 2026, App Store apps that communicate and promote offers for digital goods or services become subject to new business terms — an initial acquisition fee, a store services fee, and for apps on the EU External Purchase Link Entitlement, the Core Technology Commission. iOS 27 ships around the same date. Every vendor in your catalog will cover this with a spin toward their own product. Nobody will publish neutral arithmetic for a solo developer at three concrete revenue levels, with every figure sourced to Apple's own terms and stamped with a check date.

That is your wedge applied to the single highest-intent moment in the quarter, and it is the piece most likely to earn the first third-party citation — because it will be the only version a reader can verify. Ship it ahead of the peak, on Sep 3, then update it once the terms take effect.

Adaptation — you chose to stay anonymous

Swap personal authority for institutional authority

Four specialists recommended a named human author; you have decided against it, and this calendar respects that — every byline-dependent task is dropped, not left hanging. But the competitor study identified named accountability as a real differentiator, so it needs replacing rather than ignoring.

The substitute is the model unsigned publications have always used: accountability vested in a published process rather than a person. Three concrete assets do that work, and all three are scheduled below — a public methodology page stating exactly how prices are checked and how often, a versioned correction log that admits when a figure was wrong, and an open dataset whose git history is itself the audit trail. An anonymous site that shows its working is more credible than a named one that does not.

The honest cost: the podcast and practitioner-feature plays from the PR channel are now largely closed, and personal E-E-A-T signals are off the table. Those hours move to the methodology and dataset assets instead.

Correction — Search Console data received 2026-08-03

The "#1 for best paywall tools" claim was wrong. And the data moves the whole refresh queue.

The correction first. I previously reported that IndieAppStack ranks #1 for "best paywall tools for iOS apps," in both the competitor study and the strategy. Search Console says that page sits at average position 25.5, with 191 impressions and 1 click over 90 days. My claim came from a search tool returning the page as its first result — that is result ordering for one long-tail phrasing, not a Google ranking. It should not have been stated as a ranking, and the "the format already works" framing built on it was overconfident.

What the data actually shows. 90 days, 4,048 page impressions, 4 clicks, average position 33.4. This is a site being seen and not clicked — which at position 33 is exactly what you would expect, and is a positioning problem rather than a content-quality one.

The finding that matters most, and it is good news: the single largest intent cluster is pricing — 387 impressions across 85 queries (23% of all query impressions), best position 4.8. Real examples: codemagic pricing (50 impr, pos 10.7), superwall pricing (22, pos 10.9), fastlane pricing (32, pos 18.1), is fastlane free (13, pos 8.1). The market is already asking this site what things cost, and it is nearly ranking for it. That is empirical validation of the verifiability wedge — not a strategist's inference.

The inverse finding. "best / top" queries total just 50 impressions across 15 queries. The calendar I built was weighted toward "best X tools" guides. That was wrong, and the queue below is re-ordered around where impressions actually are: tool pages and comparisons, not guides. Comparisons earned 3 of the 4 total clicks, best position 9.0.

One technical issue the data surfaced: 16 www.indieappstack.com URLs are still accruing impressions — 368 of them, 9% of the site total, including www/tools/fastlane at 156 and www/categories/paywalls at 148 while the apex version of that category page does not appear at all. The www→apex 301 exists in the repo, so this is either propagation lag or a gap worth verifying. Either way it is splitting signal on real pages. Not a content task — flagging it for the technical backlog.

The refresh template — definition of done

Every one of the 12 refreshes below follows the same five moves

This is why a refresh costs 1.5–3 h rather than a rewrite. The per-piece entries state only what is specific to that page.

  1. Short answer block up top — restate the target query verbatim and answer it self-containedly in the first ~200 words, so it can be lifted by a retrieval engine.
  2. Dated, sourced price table — every figure links to the vendor's own pricing page and carries its own check date, not a page-level one.
  3. An explicit "when this is not the right fit" verdict — named conditions, not hedging.
  4. FAQPage schema + prompt-shaped Q&A, drawn verbatim from the 25-prompt panel the strategy stands up.
  5. Internal links up to the category hub and across to the related comparison, then re-seed Supabase and redeploy so the change is actually live.
