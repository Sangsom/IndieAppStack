♟ The play

Your differentiator is a maintenance ritual. Industrialise it, and let one ritual feed four channels.

IndieAppStack's wedge is the dated price check. Right now that wedge is three things it should not be: a one-off sweep (every tool page and guide carries the same Jul 19, 2026 date), invisible to machines (tool-page JSON-LD contains no date fields at all, so the claim exists only as prose), and unowned by any human (everything is bylined to an Organization).

Turn it into a rolling weekly ritual — re-check two tools a week, snapshot each price with its source and date — and the same hours produce four outputs: fresh, machine-readable pages for AI retrieval; the monthly newsletter; a price-change log other publications can cite; and, after two quarters of accumulation, the one piece of original research a 28-tool directory can legitimately own. That is how a person with a day job runs four channels honestly.

The strategic unlock behind it: a low-authority site cannot outrank rivals with 1,900 URLs, but it does not have to. Only ~17% of AI Overview citations now overlap the organic top 10, so retrieval can be won without the ranking — and freshness is the cheapest lever you own.

Laddering to: working goal set (no smart-goals plan exists) — North Star non-branded organic + AI-cited sessions, sequenced authority → traffic → revenue. Affiliate programs require traffic proof before approval, so revenue is a lagging outcome, not a Q1 target.

The Stack Finder promises an email that cannot be sent

Your highest-intent surface renders the CTA "Send this stack to me" with the description "Get this exact recommendation link in your inbox." I verified the code: package.json contains no email-sending dependency of any kind — no Resend, Postmark, SendGrid, Nodemailer, SES. Signups write straight to a Supabase table. Nothing has ever been sent, and deductively nothing ever could have been.

Three consequences. It is a direct contradiction of the verifiability wedge on the one page where a reader has most invested. There is no unsubscribe route anywhere in app/, and NEWSLETTER_DOUBLE_OPT_IN is unset so every address is stored as active with consent undemonstrable — live GDPR and ePrivacy exposure that begins breaching the moment a first email sends. And the capture surface is built nine times over while send capability is zero.

The copy fix is half an hour and does not wait for the ESP. Everything else in this plan assumes the brand does not make claims it cannot keep — so this is fixed first, on principle as much as on law.

Positioning

Who we're for

Solo and indie iOS developers choosing tools for a small app. Trigger: about to ship, or just shipped, an MVP and must pick monetization, backend, analytics and crash reporting without over-buying.

The wedge

Verifiability, not neutrality. The competitor study found honest "when not to use" verdicts are already published by a rival. What nobody does is make claims checkable: no competitor sources a price or dates a figure.

Value proposition

The only indie tool guide where every price carries a source and a check date — and where the answer is sometimes "do not buy this", including when the affiliate link pays.

Category frame

Existing category, new standard. Do not invent a category; instead define the bar for the one that exists — an audited guide in a market of undated listicles.

Message hierarchy

  1. Primary promise: Every price dated. Every claim sourced. Including when the answer is "don't buy".
  2. Proof: a visible check date and source link on all 26 indexable tool pages; a public methodology page; a named human author; a published correction log; at least one verdict against a tool that pays commission.
  3. Per-persona angles: Pre-launch — what is the minimum stack, and what can wait? Just shipped — what do I add now, and at what real cost? Cost-conscious — which tier am I over-paying for, and when does the free tier end?

Roadmap

Wave 0 · next 2 weeks

Stop the bleeding, unblock the channel

~8 h total
  • Fix the Stack Finder copy so it no longer promises an email that cannot be sent. 0.5 h — do this first.
  • Ship an RSS feed. Seven candidate paths were probed and all 404. Without a feed the single best distribution play below is inert. 2–3 h
  • Put a named human on the site — an /about that names you, links an app you shipped, and describes how the price-check process actually works. 2–3 h
  • One GitHub PR to the iOS Dev Directory. 1–2 h — the highest-leverage hour in the entire plan; see below.
Wave 1 · Q3 2026

Make the wedge checkable by machines — and measurable

~45–60 h
  • Stand up the measurement rig before anything else: a 25-prompt panel re-run monthly, a GA4 custom channel group isolating ChatGPT / Perplexity / Claude / Gemini / Copilot referrals, and Bing Webmaster verification to unlock its free AI Performance Report. Without this every later initiative is unfalsifiable.
  • Add a pricing_snapshots table. Urgent purely because of the clock: the schema currently holds a single overwritable pricing_last_checked, so price history is being destroyed on every edit and the original-research asset cannot begin accumulating until this lands.
  • Emit the wedge as structured data — offers / PriceSpecification with the check date, and an honest dateModified, across all 26 indexable tool pages.
  • Begin the rolling re-check ritual — 2 tools/week, so the whole catalog cycles inside ~13 weeks.
  • Refresh the four published-but-invisible pages (ASO tools, Supabase vs Firebase, Superwall vs RevenueCat, privacy analytics) into answer-shaped pages with a Short answer block, a dated sourced price table and an explicit when-not-to-use verdict. Refresh, do not write a fifth.
  • Wire a real ESP with double opt-in, one-click unsubscribe, and a re-permission pass over the existing list; update the privacy policy to name GA4 and every processor.
  • Self-list where the brand is absent — AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, Indie Hackers — and separately correct the record: create the missing Superwall entry on AlternativeTo (its page is a genuine 404) and add StoreKit-native alternatives above Stripe on the RevenueCat page.
Wave 2 · Q4 2026

Own the queries the incumbents get wrong

~40–50 h
  • Publish the uncontested pages: "Superwall alternatives" (nobody owns it) and "why Stripe is not a RevenueCat alternative on iOS" — a checkable factual correction, since Apple mandates StoreKit IAP for in-app digital goods.
  • Retro-fit prompt-shaped Q&A blocks and FAQPage schema onto the 10 guides and 7 comparisons, sourced verbatim from the prompt panel. ~2 pages/week, not as a block.
  • Ship the monthly verification log — framed as "prices checked, prices moved", so a month where nothing changed is still a credible issue rather than padding.
  • Run the newsletter submission loop — iOS Dev Weekly's form explicitly accepts "you wrote it", which removes the self-promotion problem entirely.
  • Vendor fact-check outreach to all 28 vendors: "we list your price as X, checked on Y — confirm or correct." The only vendor motion that strengthens editorial standing rather than spending it.
  • Product Hunt launch — but email them first; eligibility for a curated content site is genuinely ambiguous in their published guidance and it is a one-shot.
Wave 3 · Q1–Q2 2027

Convert the ritual into a compounding asset

~35–45 h
  • Publish the Indie iOS Tool Pricing Index — two quarters of snapshots become the only original research a directory can legitimately own: what indie tooling actually costs and how it moved, every figure dated and sourced. Ship as a page plus versioned CSV/JSON on GitHub with Dataset schema; the public git history is itself the proof of the claim.
  • Internal-link and hub consolidation so the 12 category hubs become the canonical head-term targets once authority arrives.
  • Practitioner features — pitch yourself as an indie developer, not the site. Indie Dev Monday and Empower Apps both feature practitioners; the site gets mentioned as a consequence, not as the ask.

Measurement plan

How we'll know it's working — and rebalance

Reviewed weekly on leading inputs, rebalanced quarterly on lagging outcomes.

  • ▸Foundation. GA4 (G-Y7CVEVCJ7H) is the sole provider and currently runs with no consent banner — a live GDPR exposure that should be resolved before this plan drives new traffic. Note the consequence for measurement: once a banner lands, the newsletter_signup event will undercount, so the ESP becomes the system of record for signups, not GA4. Record a pre-banner baseline now.
  • ▸Triangulation — and an honest limit. The standard three-lens model (platform attribution + incrementality + MMM) does not apply here. There is no spend to model and nothing to hold out. The correct tier is platform attribution only: GA4 + Search Console + Bing Webmaster Tools, plus the manual prompt panel. Anyone proposing MMM for a $0 plan is selling something.
  • ▸GEO / AI answer engines. A fixed 25-prompt panel run monthly and logged, scored per engine rather than blended — only ~11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity, so one engine does not proxy the others. Baseline: cited in 1 of 8 category prompts (2026-08-03). Deliberately manual: every AI-visibility tracker found costs $99–500/month, which buys nothing 25 prompts a month does not.
  • ▸Cadence & rebalancing rule. Weekly: pieces shipped vs the 2-new + 4-refresh plan, tools re-checked. Quarterly: if third-party mentions are still zero after two quarters, PR has failed and its hours move to content. If the prompt-panel rate has not moved after two quarters of fresh, structured pages, the freshness thesis is wrong and the plan needs re-basing. Social tripwires: reopen the skip only if iosdev.space activity roughly quadruples, or any social domain organically sends >2% of sessions with zero posting.

Deliberately not doing this cycle

  • ✕Paid media. No budget — and it fails on effectiveness independently of cost: Muck Rack's May 2026 analysis of 25M+ links found paid and advertorial content drives just 0.3% of AI citations against 84% from earned media. There is no version of this with money that would change the recommendation much.
  • ✕Brand social accounts, on any platform. The measured ceiling is the argument: iosdev.space has 259 monthly active users, the best-known Apple-platform Bluesky accounts sit at 1–5k followers, and the iOS-dev starter packs show 0–2 all-time joins. A brand account needs 2–4 h/week to not look abandoned — 20–50% of the entire budget on the lowest-ceiling channel, in a voice (no contractions, no emoji, no superlatives) that feed algorithms actively suppress. Skipping buys back those hours.
  • ✕The sameAs social-schema ticket — descope it. It is a recommended, not required, property and its absence costs approximately nothing. The real entity problem is zero third-party mentions, which sameAs cannot fix because it is self-assertion, not corroboration. If an anchor is wanted, point it at a GitHub org or Wikidata, never at a dormant social profile.
  • ✕New tool pages or catalog expansion. 72 URLs at zero authority already risks thin-content dilution. Rivals have 339–1,926 URLs; volume is a race you cannot win and should not enter.
  • ✕Show HN for the directory. Its published rules explicitly exclude "blog posts, sign-up pages, newsletters, lists, and other reading material" — submitting it invites flagging and risks the domain. The open pricing dataset is the artifact that would qualify, later.
  • ✕Gating the six stack-archetype pages behind email. They are indexed, crawlable, AI-citable assets serving the North Star directly. Gating them trades the primary goal for a handful of addresses.
  • ✕More email capture surfaces. Capture already renders on nine surfaces; send capability is zero. Nothing about this is a capture problem.
  • ✕Any vendor co-marketing that trades editorial independence — link-for-affiliate-rate deals, paid featured placement, pre-publication vendor review. In a small, technical, sceptical niche the damage is irreversible, and the wedge is the only differentiated asset the site has. Rule to adopt: never hold an affiliate relationship and a co-marketing arrangement with the same vendor.
  • ✕Chasing RevenueCat's data moat (115,000 apps of telemetry) or localising. Neither is reachable, and the Pricing Index is the version of "original research" that actually is.
