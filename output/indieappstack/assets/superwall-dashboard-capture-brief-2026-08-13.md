# Capture brief: Superwall paywall editor and campaign or placement

**Status:** brief-only  
**Production path:** capture-or-source; real product UI; never generate  
**Source piece:** Superwall alternatives for indie iOS apps  
**Placement:** After the “Short answer” section  
**Final filename:** `superwall-paywall-editor-campaign-placement.png`

## Editorial purpose

Provide first-party visual evidence for the article’s description of Superwall as a paywall presentation and experimentation layer. The image must show the real product, not a reconstruction, and should help a builder see the relationship between a paywall and the campaign or placement that controls when it appears.

## Exact screen and claim

Capture a current Superwall dashboard view that shows:

- the paywall editor or a paywall preview clearly enough to identify the editing workflow; and
- the associated campaign or placement configuration, either in the same screen or as a 2-panel editorial composite made from 2 real captures.

Claim supported: “Superwall can own paywall presentation and experiments while another system, such as RevenueCat, retains purchase and entitlement truth.” The screenshot only needs to evidence Superwall’s editor and campaign/placement workflow. It must not imply that the capture proves the separate RevenueCat integration unless that integration is visibly and accurately configured.

## Capture instructions

1. Use a current, authorized Superwall account or a vendor-provided demo/press asset.
2. Set the dashboard to its light theme, if available, to sit cleanly on IndieAppStack’s paper background.
3. Use a desktop browser at 1440 × 900 CSS pixels or larger and capture at 2× device scale. The source should therefore be at least 2880 pixels wide before editorial cropping.
4. Prefer a native PNG capture. Do not resize upward, add artificial sharpness, or redraw interface elements.
5. Keep the product navigation and relevant screen title visible so the context is legible.
6. If the editor and campaign/placement cannot appear together, capture each screen separately at the same browser size and join them in a simple 2-panel composite. Use only IndieAppStack’s rule color (`#e7e2d7`) as the divider; do not alter either UI.
7. Crop to the smallest frame that preserves the editor, the campaign/placement relationship, and enough dashboard chrome to establish authenticity.

## Privacy and security redactions

Before export, remove or blur all private or misleading data:

- workspace, app, project, bundle, and team names;
- customer identifiers, email addresses, device identifiers, and user attributes;
- API keys, tokens, webhook URLs, integration secrets, and environment values;
- revenue, conversion, subscriber, or experiment results that are private, synthetic, or not used by the article;
- internal campaign names, unpublished product names, and test notes.

Use a flat neutral rectangle or irreversible raster blur. Do not leave readable data beneath translucent overlays. Keep generic interface labels visible whenever they are necessary to understand the workflow.

## Editorial treatment

- Do not add a Superwall logo or marketing claim over the UI.
- Do not add arrows, “winner” badges, percentages, or annotations not present in the product.
- A small IndieAppStack caption may sit outside the image in the article; it should not be baked into the screenshot.
- Preserve native colors. Do not recolor the Superwall interface to the IndieAppStack palette.
- Record the account plan, app version if exposed, capture date, and screen route privately in the asset log so the image can be recaptured when the interface changes.
